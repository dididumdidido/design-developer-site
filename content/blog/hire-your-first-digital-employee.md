The biggest mistake I see?

Trying to automate everything at once.

Ten tools. Twenty workflows. One very tired owner trying to keep it all running.

Your first digital employee should do ONE job really well. Here's how I'd pick it.

## 1. List the work that keeps losing to the urgent

Grab a piece of paper. Write down five things that need to happen every week but keep slipping.

The follow-ups. The lead that came in after hours. The blog post you meant to write. The inbox that's waiting for you at 6 a.m.

That list is your hiring list.

## 2. Pick the one that costs you the most

Not the most annoying one. The most expensive one.

For a lot of businesses, it's lead response. You paid for that lead. If nobody answers for a day, they book with someone else.

That one stings.

## 3. Write the job description

Keep it short. One paragraph.

- What it owns.
- Where it works (your inbox, your CRM, your calendar).
- What it should never do without asking you first.

Simple rule: if you wouldn't hand it to a first-week temp, don't hand it to your digital employee yet either.

## 4. Set one KPI

One. Not five.

- Lead Response: time to first reply, and calls booked.
- Executive Assistant: inbox at zero by a set time each day.
- SEO Manager: articles published per month.

The KPI is how you'll know it's working. And how it knows what "done" looks like.

## 5. Give it a seat on your org board

Put it on the chart. Who does it report to? Who does it hand work to?

This sounds small. It isn't. When it has a seat, your team knows where its work goes, and it stops being "that AI thing nobody checks."

## 6. Review it weekly

Fifteen minutes a week. Look at the KPI. Read a few of its outputs. Correct what's off.

Most are built in about 2 weeks, and results usually show in 30 to 90 days depending on the role. It gets better every week as it learns your business, your customers and what works.

## 7. Then hire the next one

Once the first one is running without you, pick the next item on your list.

That's how you build a team that never clocks out. One seat at a time.
