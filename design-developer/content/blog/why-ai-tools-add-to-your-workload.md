They promised to save you time.

Then you spent your Sunday setting them up. Then you spent Monday checking what they did. Then Tuesday fixing it.

Sound familiar?

You're not doing it wrong. Most AI tools are built that way.

## Tools need an operator

A tool waits for you.

You open it. You prompt it. You copy the answer somewhere else. You remember to do it again tomorrow.

That's not delegation. That's a new task with a nicer interface.

And every new tool adds another login, another tab, another thing your team has to remember to use.

## The problem isn't the AI. It's the job.

When nothing owns the outcome, you do.

That's why the workload goes up. The AI did a piece of the work, but nobody is responsible for finishing it. So it lands back on your desk.

## What to do instead: give it a job

Here's the shift.

Stop asking "which tool should I try?"

Ask WHO should own this.

Then build that "who." A digital employee with a role, a KPI and a seat on your org board. It works the queue end to end and reports back, like any hire would.

- The lead gets answered and booked, not just answered.
- The article gets written AND published, not just drafted.
- The follow-up actually goes out, and you see that it did.

## Three signs you need a digital employee, not another tool

1. **You're the one checking the AI's work every day.**
2. **The same task keeps slipping, no matter how many tools you add.**
3. **Your team has stopped using the tools you paid for.**

If any of those hit home, it's not a tool problem. It's a role nobody has filled.
